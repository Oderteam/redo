Vision

"The work happens right where they meet, somewhere between possibility and form, between spontaneity and composition."

Tim Oder's practice treats art as the space where order and chaos meet. Structure and gesture, line and mark, plan and accident are two ways of asking the same question. The works, the research and the studio all move from that one idea.

Order

"Order is a tool, not a goal. It holds a piece together just long enough for it to be seen."

In the HardEdge works, structure comes first: the grid, the plane, the repeated element. It isn't the goal. It's the scaffolding that lets a piece hold still long enough to be seen.

Chaos

"Chaos is a medium, full of possibility. Approached with attention, it gives you things you couldn't have planned."

The Asemic works start from that same idea: writing without settling into words, a mark made without knowing exactly where it will land. The mistake isn't corrected out. It's read as information.

View Asemic works →

Order and chaos don't stay in their own corners for long. Every HardEdge piece has a moment where the grid slips. Every Asemic mark is still, underneath, held by a hand that knows what it's doing. The crossing point between them is where the real work happens.

Club 31

An ongoing research into order and chaos, one piece at a time, one theme at a time. Not a finished collection. A live investigation, with a new work arriving every few months, made for whoever is following closely enough to want to hold one.
